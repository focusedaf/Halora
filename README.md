# HALORA

### Hybrid Framework for Hallucination Detection in Large Language Models Using Internal Signals and Retrieval-Grounded Verification

> **HALORA** is a hybrid hallucination detection framework designed to identify, verify, and analyze hallucinated content generated by Large Language Models (LLMs).

---

## Overview

Large Language Models are capable of generating highly fluent and convincing responses, but they can also produce information that is factually incorrect, unsupported, fabricated, or unverifiable.

Hallucinations can take different forms, including:

- Incorrect factual claims
- Unsupported statements
- Inconsistent responses
- Fabricated citations
- Incorrect bibliographic information
- Invalid or non-existent URLs

Existing hallucination detection approaches often focus on a single aspect of the problem. Some analyze internal model representations, while others rely on external evidence, behavioral consistency, citation verification, or URL validation.

**HALORA aims to bring these complementary approaches together within a unified framework.**

The framework adapts its detection strategy according to the **accessibility of the target model** and the **characteristics of its generated output**.

---

## What HALORA Does

HALORA combines several complementary detection and verification mechanisms:

### Internal-Signal Detection

For open-source / white-box models, HALORA investigates internal model representations such as hidden states and activations to identify signals associated with hallucination.

### Behavioral Detection

For closed-source / black-box models where internal representations are inaccessible, HALORA investigates output-level behavior through techniques such as response resampling, perturbation, and consistency analysis.

### Factual Verification

Generated responses are decomposed into atomic claims and checked against retrieved external evidence to determine whether claims are supported, contradicted, or unverifiable.

### Citation Verification

Generated citations and references are independently analyzed for bibliographic validity, metadata consistency, and supporting evidence.

### URL Verification

URLs generated by an LLM are checked for validity, accessibility, and potentially stale or non-resolving resources.

### Multilingual Evaluation

HALORA evaluates its detection and verification mechanisms across multiple languages to investigate language-specific performance and cross-language transferability.

### Evidence Fusion

Outputs from the applicable detection and verification modules are combined into a unified representation and processed through a proposed XGBoost meta-classifier to produce a final hallucination assessment.
